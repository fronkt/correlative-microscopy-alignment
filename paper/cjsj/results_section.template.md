# Results

## The retained fraction flags failures (H1)
MatchAnything-RoMa registered {{n_success_primary}} of the {{n_pairs}} pairs to within 20 pixels; a homography fitted to the annotated points could have registered {{ceiling20}}. Its retained fraction separated the successes from the failures with an AUROC of {{auc_s1}} (95 % confidence interval {{auc_s1_lo}} to {{auc_s1_hi}}), meeting the pre-registered bar of 0.80, and {{auc_s1_8}} at the 8-pixel criterion of Durmaz et al., who reported 0.905 on 48 pairs [2]. For RoMa the AUROC was {{auc_s1_roma}}. Within each of the {{ex_grp_k}} task groups that had both successes and failures, the AUROC ranged from {{ex_grp_min}} to {{ex_grp_max}} (exploratory; Fig. 2); none of the {{ex_slip_n}} slip-partitioning pairs was registered and all {{ex_frac_n}} fracture-surface pairs were.

The transfer test was mixed. The cut-off chosen on the {{design_pairs}} same-slice and serial-sectioning pairs, a retained fraction of {{cutoff}}, was applied unchanged to the {{held_pairs}} pairs of the other four groups. It accepted {{held_acc_n}} of them and kept every correct registration (recall {{held_recall}}), and the share of accepted registrations that were correct was {{held_acc_succ}} against {{held_base}} for all of them (difference {{held_diff}} percentage points, confidence interval {{held_diff_lo}} to {{held_diff_hi}}), which passes the pre-registered test. But the cut-off also accepted {{held_far}} of the failures in the new groups, and {{ex_fa_tem}} of these {{ex_fa}} false accepts were dislocation images from the transmission electron microscope ({{ex_fa_tem}} of that group's {{ex_tem_fail}} failures; exploratory). On these pairs the fit kept a large share of the correspondences while placing the image hundreds of pixels away (Fig. 2), possibly because, as Durmaz et al. note, dislocations visible under one diffraction condition can be invisible under the other, which leaves the two images few features in common [2]. A cut-off fitted on other kinds of pairs is therefore too lenient for this kind, even though the ranking holds within it.

## Triage
Ranking all pairs by the retained fraction and accepting only the top quarter raised the share of correct registrations from {{tri_primary_S1_base}} to {{tri_primary_S1_25}} (confidence interval {{tri_primary_S1_25_lo}} to {{tri_primary_S1_25_hi}}); accepting the top half gave {{tri_primary_S1_50}} (Fig. 3a). A researcher who checks registrations from the lowest-scoring upward therefore finds most of the failures first.

## Agreement did not help (H2)
Agreement between registrations predicted MatchAnything-RoMa's success less well than the retained fraction (AUROC {{auc_s2}}; difference from the retained fraction {{pd_s2}} over all candidates, confidence interval {{pd_s2_lo}} to {{pd_s2_hi}}), and the combined score did not improve on it either (pooled difference {{pd_s3}}, interval {{pd_s3_lo}} to {{pd_s3_hi}}). H2 is not supported. One reason is that the methods tend to fail together. On {{ex_none}} pairs not one of the {{n_voters}} registrations succeeded, and of the {{ex_cons}} pairs on which at least half of the registrations agreed to within 20 pixels, {{ex_cons_fail}} had no correct registration at all (exploratory).

## Choosing per pair did not beat the best single method (H3)
The best single method, MatchAnything-RoMa inside the zoom search, registered {{best_single_n}} pairs. Choosing per pair by the retained fraction registered {{sel_S1_n}} ({{sel_S1_won}} pairs won, {{sel_S1_lost}} lost, McNemar *p* = {{sel_S1_p}}), by agreement {{sel_S2_n}}, and by the combined score {{sel_S3_n}} (Fig. 3b). H3 is not supported, although an oracle that always picked a correct registration when one existed would have registered {{oracle_n}}.

Two exploratory analyses, decided after these results and therefore only suggestive, point to why. First, the retained fraction is not comparable across matchers: SIFT proposes far fewer correspondences (median {{ex_sift_nm}}, against a fixed 10,000 for the RoMa family), and a fit to few points keeps a larger share of them (median retained fraction {{ex_sift_med}} against {{ex_dense_med}}). Choosing by the retained fraction picked SIFT on {{ex_sift_picks}} pairs, and {{ex_lost_sift}} of the {{ex_lost}} pairs it lost were SIFT picks. Within a single pair, the fraction usually does prefer a correct registration over a failed one (median within-pair AUROC {{ex_within}} over the {{ex_mixed}} pairs that had both). Choosing only among the {{ex_dense_k}} registrations from the RoMa family registered {{ex_dense_n}} pairs ({{ex_dense_won}} won, {{ex_dense_lost}} lost, *p* = {{ex_dense_p}}), which needs its own pre-registered test. Second, much of the oracle's advantage is fragile: of the {{ex_oracle_only}} pairs only the oracle registered, {{ex_oracle_le2}} were registered by at most two of the {{n_voters}} methods, and on {{ex_flip}} pairs MatchAnything-RoMa itself succeeded in some of six reruns and failed in others.

# Discussion

Every registration pipeline that uses robust fitting already computes the retained fraction, so this triage costs nothing. On the full benchmark it separates good from bad registrations about as well as Durmaz et al. found on four subsets [2], in every task group where this could be tested, and ranking a batch by it puts most failures at the bottom of the list. Three cautions follow from the negative results. A fixed cut-off learned on one kind of image pair can admit many failures on another, as it did for transmission electron microscope images, so the fraction is better used to rank a batch, or with a cut-off set for each kind of image, than as a universal threshold. It should not be compared across matchers that propose very different numbers of correspondences. And agreement among several registrations is not a check on correctness when the methods tend to fail together.

The study has limits. It uses one benchmark, whose {{n_scenes}} scenes make the confidence intervals wide. Each registration was run once, and the reruns show that random sampling alone moves a few pairs across the 20-pixel line. The 20-pixel criterion is a choice; results at 8 pixels are given for comparison with earlier work. The input transformations were chosen for simplicity, not tuned.

For materials scientists using automatic registration, the practical message is simple: keep the retained fraction, sort by it, and check the lowest-scoring registrations first.
