For MatchAnything-RoMa, the retained fraction separated successes from failures with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of {{auc_s1}} ({{auc_s1_8}} at the stricter 8-pixel criterion of earlier work), and accepting only the highest-scoring quarter of pairs raised the share of correct registrations from {{tri_primary_S1_base}} to {{tri_primary_S1_25}}. # Results

## The retained fraction flags failures (H1)
MatchAnything-RoMa registered {{n_success_primary}} of the {{n_pairs}} pairs to within 20 pixels. A homography fitted to the annotated points reached {{ceiling20}}, an optimistic ceiling because it is scored on the points it was fitted to. The retained fraction separated MatchAnything-RoMa's successes from its failures with an AUROC of {{auc_s1}} (95 % confidence interval {{auc_s1_lo}} to {{auc_s1_hi}}), meeting the pre-registered bar of 0.80. At 10 pixels the AUROC was {{auc_s1_10}} ({{auc_s1_10_lo}} to {{auc_s1_10_hi}}), and at the 8-pixel criterion of Durmaz et al., who reported 0.905 on 48 pairs [2], it was {{auc_s1_8}} ({{auc_s1_8_lo}} to {{auc_s1_8_hi}}). For RoMa, a secondary comparison, it was {{auc_s1_roma}}. Within the {{ex_grp_k}} task groups that had both successes and failures, the AUROC ranged from {{ex_grp_min}} to {{ex_grp_max}} (exploratory; the highest from 13 pairs). None of the {{ex_slip_n}} slip-partitioning pairs was registered, and all {{ex_frac_n}} fracture-surface pairs were (Fig. 2).

The cut-off chosen on the {{design_pairs}} same-slice and serial-sectioning pairs, a retained fraction of {{cutoff}}, was applied unchanged to the {{held_pairs}} pairs of the other four groups. It accepted {{held_acc_n}} of them and kept every correct registration. Of the accepted registrations, {{held_acc_succ}} were correct, against {{held_base}} of all registrations in these groups (difference {{held_diff}} percentage points, confidence interval {{held_diff_lo}} to {{held_diff_hi}}), which passes the pre-registered test.

The pass is weak. The share correct among accepted registrations fell from {{design_prec}} in the groups used to choose the cut-off to {{held_acc_succ}}; the interval is wide because the {{held_pairs}} pairs come from only {{ex_held_scenes}} scenes; and the cut-off accepted {{held_far}} of the failures. All {{ex_fa}} wrongly accepted pairs were TEM images of dislocations in one alloy, MoTaTiZrHf, from {{ex_fa_scenes}} scenes (exploratory); {{ex_tem_alloy_n}} of the benchmark's {{ex_tem_n}} TEM pairs come from this alloy. On these pairs the fit kept a large share of the correspondences but missed by {{ex_fa_min}} to {{ex_fa_max}} pixels (median {{ex_fa_med}}; Fig. 1c, Fig. 2): the matches agreed with one another but not with the truth. The cause was not tested, and because only one alloy is involved, it may lie in the specimen rather than the microscope.

## Triage
Ranking all pairs by the retained fraction and accepting the top quarter raised the share of correct registrations from {{tri_primary_S1_base}} to {{tri_primary_S1_25}} (confidence interval {{tri_primary_S1_25_lo}} to {{tri_primary_S1_25_hi}}); accepting the top half or top three quarters gave {{tri_primary_S1_50}} and {{tri_primary_S1_75}} (Fig. 3a). The area under the risk-coverage curve, the average failure rate among accepted registrations over all acceptance levels, was {{aurc}} ({{aurc_lo}} to {{aurc_hi}}), against {{aurc_random}} for a random order. In exploratory counts, the {{ex_bottom_fail}} lowest-scoring registrations were all failures, and every correct registration ranked in the top {{ex_lowest_succ}}. At the other end, {{ex_topq_fail}} of the {{ex_topq_n}} registrations in the top quarter were failures, all from the same TEM alloy.

## Agreement did not improve prediction (H2)
For MatchAnything-RoMa, agreement alone gave an AUROC of {{auc_s2}}, lower than the retained fraction by {{d_s2}} ({{d_s2_lo}} to {{d_s2_hi}}), and the combined score gave {{auc_s3}}, a difference of {{d_s3}} ({{d_s3_lo}} to {{d_s3_hi}}). Pooled over all {{n_voters}} candidates, the combined score differed from the retained fraction by {{pd_s3}} ({{pd_s3_lo}} to {{pd_s3_hi}}). H2 required the combined score to do better. It did not in either comparison, and for MatchAnything-RoMa it did significantly worse. One reason is that the candidates were not independent: {{ex_dense_voters}} of the {{n_voters}} are variants of RoMa, and they tend to fail together. On {{ex_none}} pairs no candidate succeeded, and of the {{ex_cons}} pairs on which at least half of the candidates agreed to within 20 pixels, {{ex_cons_fail}} had no correct candidate at all (exploratory).

## Choosing per pair did not beat the best single method (H3)
The best single method, MatchAnything-RoMa inside the zoom search, registered {{best_single_n}} pairs. Choosing per pair by the retained fraction registered {{sel_S1_n}} ({{sel_S1_won}} pairs gained, {{sel_S1_lost}} lost, McNemar *p* = {{sel_S1_p}}; difference {{sel_S1_gain_lo}} to {{sel_S1_gain_hi}} percentage points), by agreement {{sel_S2_n}}, and by the combined score {{sel_S3_n}} (Fig. 3b). H3 is not supported. An oracle that always picked a correct registration when one existed would have registered {{oracle_n}}. As a control, picking the best of six runs of one matcher by the retained fraction gained {{rerun_ma_n}} pairs for MatchAnything-RoMa and {{rerun_roma_n}} for RoMa.

Exploratory follow-ups suggest two reasons. First, the retained fraction is not comparable across matchers. SIFT proposed far fewer correspondences (median {{ex_sift_nm}}, against 10,000 for the RoMa family), and a fit to few points keeps a larger share of them (median retained fraction {{ex_sift_med}} against {{ex_dense_med}}). Choosing by the retained fraction picked SIFT on {{ex_sift_picks}} pairs, and {{ex_lost_sift}} of the {{ex_lost}} pairs it lost were SIFT picks. Within a single pair, the fraction usually did prefer a correct registration over a failed one (median within-pair AUROC {{ex_within}} over the {{ex_mixed}} pairs that had both), and choosing only among the {{ex_dense_k}} RoMa-family candidates registered {{ex_dense_n}} pairs ({{ex_dense_won}} gained, {{ex_dense_lost}} lost, *p* = {{ex_dense_p}}); that rule needs its own pre-registered test. Second, much of the oracle's lead rests on unstable successes. Of the {{ex_oracle_only}} pairs that only the oracle registered, {{ex_oracle_le2}} were registered by at most two of the {{n_voters}} candidates. Across its six runs, MatchAnything-RoMa registered {{ex_ma_ever}} different pairs at least once, and {{ex_flip}} of those succeeded in some runs and failed in others.

# Discussion

Any registration pipeline that uses robust fitting already computes the retained fraction, so using it needs no extra computation. On the full benchmark it separated good from bad registrations as well as Durmaz et al. found on four subsets [2]. Ranking by it was most reliable at the bottom of the list: here the lowest-scoring registrations could have been discarded or rerun without losing a single correct one. The top of the list was less safe. A cut-off learned on one kind of image pair accepted many confident failures on another kind, the TEM images of one alloy, so the fraction should be calibrated for each kind of image, or its high scores checked by hand on a new kind, before it is trusted. It should not be compared across matchers that propose very different numbers of correspondences, and agreement among variants of one matcher is not a check on correctness.

The study uses one benchmark; its {{n_scenes}} scenes make the confidence intervals wide, and the transfer test rests on {{ex_held_scenes}} scenes. Each candidate was run once, and MatchAnything-RoMa's six runs registered between {{ex_seed_min}} and {{ex_seed_max}} pairs, so the best single method, chosen on the same data, led the next candidates by less than this run-to-run spread. The 20-pixel criterion was applied to wide images from {{ex_w_min}} to {{ex_w_max}} pixels across, so it is stricter for larger images; results at 8 and 10 pixels are given. The image transformations were chosen for simplicity, not tuned.

For materials scientists using automatic registration, these results support a simple procedure: record the retained fraction, sort a batch by it, rerun or discard the lowest-scoring registrations, and check the high-scoring ones by hand on any kind of image for which the score has not yet been validated.
